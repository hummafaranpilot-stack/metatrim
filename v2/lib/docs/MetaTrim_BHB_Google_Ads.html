3. Before & After

COMPLIANCE NOTE -- DO NOT USE TRADITIONAL BEFORE/AFTER ON GOOGLE ADS

Google Ads policy explicitly restricts before-and-after imagery for weight loss products. This includes:

  • Side-by-side body comparison photos

  • Progress photos showing body changes

  • Any imagery implying guaranteed physical transformation

  • "Before" and "after" labels on any body imagery


Violation consequences: Ad disapproval, account suspension, or permanent ban from advertising weight loss supplements on Google.

Reference: Google Ads Healthcare and Medicines Policy; Misrepresentation Policy; Weight Loss section.

9. Advertorial

NOTE ON GOOGLE ADS + ADVERTORIAL FORMAT

Advertorial-style landing pages are NOT standard for Search or Display campaigns. Google may flag them under Misrepresentation policies if they mimic news sites without clear disclosure.

Where advertorials CAN work on Google:

  • Demand Gen campaigns (feeds into Discovery, Gmail, YouTube)

  • Performance Max (when used as a landing page with proper disclosure)


Requirements for compliance:
  • Must include "ADVERTISEMENT" or "SPONSORED CONTENT" label clearly at top

  • Cannot mimic a specific news outlet's branding

  • Must not use fake journalist bylines

  • All claims must include appropriate qualifiers

    • Pre-Launch Checklist

    Run through every item before submitting any ad for MetaTrim BHB (or any supplement) on Google Ads.

    Ad Copy Compliance

    • [ ] No guaranteed results. Never say "you WILL lose weight" or "guaranteed to work." Use "supports," "helps," "promotes," "may assist."
    • [ ] No specific weight/size claims. Never say "lose 10 pounds" or "drop 2 dress sizes." No numbers tied to weight loss outcomes.
    • [ ] No before/after body imagery. No side-by-side comparison photos. No progress pics. No body measurement visuals.
    • [ ] No "miracle" or "cure" language. Avoid: miracle, breakthrough cure, magic pill, secret formula, guaranteed fix.
    • [ ] No disease claims. Cannot claim to treat, cure, diagnose, or prevent any disease. This includes diabetes, heart disease, obesity (classified as a disease), etc.
    • [ ] Qualifier language included. Every claim is softened: "may help support," "designed to promote," "intended to assist."
    • [ ] "Results may vary" disclaimer present. Required on all testimonial-based content.
    • [ ] FDA disclaimer included on landing page. "These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

    Landing Page Compliance

    • [ ] No misleading claims on landing page. Google reviews the landing page, not just the ad. Everything on the page must comply.
    • [ ] Clear product identification. The product name, what it is (dietary supplement), and the company must be clearly stated.
    • [ ] Ingredient list visible. Full supplement facts panel should be accessible on the landing page.
    • [ ] Return policy clearly stated. The 365-day guarantee should be easy to find.
    • [ ] No fake urgency. Avoid fake countdown timers, "only 3 left in stock" lies, or manufactured scarcity.
    • [ ] No fake endorsements. Do not attribute quotes to doctors, celebrities, or organizations without verifiable permission.
    • [ ] Privacy policy and terms of service linked. Required for all e-commerce landing pages on Google Ads.
    • [ ] Contact information available. Physical address or clear customer service contact method.

    Account & Campaign Level

    • [ ] Google Merchant Center approved (if using Shopping). Supplement products must pass Merchant Center review.
    • [ ] Healthcare & Medicines certification (if required in your region). Some countries require pre-certification. Check Google's country-specific requirements.
    • [ ] Remarketing restrictions noted. Google restricts remarketing for health-related categories. Cannot build audiences based on health conditions.
    • [ ] Age targeting set appropriately. Target 35+ for women, 40+ for men per product positioning. Do not target under 18.
    • [ ] Placement exclusions set. Exclude sensitive content categories, children's content, and inappropriate placements.

    If Your Ad Gets Disapproved

  • Read the specific policy violation cited in the disapproval notice
  • Check both the ad copy AND the landing page -- Google reviews both
  • Remove or reword the flagged language
  • Resubmit for review
  • If incorrectly flagged, submit a manual appeal through Google Ads support
  • Document all disapprovals and resolutions for future reference
  • Do NOT create a new ad account to circumvent disapprovals -- this will get you permanently banned

    • Final Notes for Media Buyers

    • Test creative types independently. Don't mix UGC and polished animation in the same ad group during testing. Isolate variables.
    • Rotate hooks aggressively. The first 5 seconds determine 80% of your YouTube ad performance. Test new hooks weekly.
    • Landing page matters more than ad copy. Google reviews the full experience. A compliant ad pointing to a non-compliant page will still get disapproved.
    • The 365-day guarantee is your strongest angle. Lead with it. It reduces perceived risk and outperforms price-first messaging in testing.
    • "No caffeine, no stimulants" resonates with 40+ audiences. This demographic has been burned by stimulant-heavy supplements. Call it out early and often.
    • Price anchoring works. "$31/bottle" is meaningless without context. Compare to daily coffee cost ($4-6/day vs ~$1/day for MetaTrim) in longer-form content.
    • Demand Gen is your best channel for advertorial content. Search and Display are stricter. Demand Gen allows more editorial-style formats.
    • Keep testimonials real and varied. Mix ages, genders, and specific experiences. Generic praise performs worse than specific, relatable stories.
